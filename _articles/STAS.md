article_type: "Evidence brief"

question: >-
  Should spread through air spaces (STAS) be incorporated as a histologic
  descriptor in the Ninth Edition of the TNM Classification of Lung Cancer,
  alongside visceral pleural invasion (VPI) and lymphovascular invasion (LVI)?

background: >-
  STAS is defined as lung cancer tumor cells identified microscopically beyond
  the edge of the main tumor in the surrounding alveolar parenchyma. It has
  been reported in 20% to 40% of surgically resected lung cancers and is
  associated with poor prognosis across all major histologic types, including
  adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, small cell carcinoma, large cell
  neuroendocrine carcinoma, atypical carcinoid, and pleomorphic carcinoma.
  Multiple investigators have proposed incorporating STAS into the TNM staging
  system in various ways—as tumor size, residual tumor (R) status, or a
  histologic descriptor that could upstage T1 tumors to T2a, similar to VPI.
  However, its role in lung cancer staging had not been established. The IASLC
  Staging and Prognostic Factors Committee therefore investigated STAS in
  completely resected (R0) stage I (T1/T2a N0M0) NSCLC using the IASLC ninth
  edition staging database.

evidence:
  - source: "IASLC Staging Project, Travis et al. (2024); 4061 pStage I R0 NSCLC"
    finding: "STAS was found in 930 of 4061 cases (22.9%). Patients with STAS had significantly worse recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS) in both univariate and multivariable analyses across all NSCLC, adenocarcinoma, other NSCLC, lobectomy, and sublobar resection cohorts. Three-year RFS was 91% without STAS vs 80% with STAS; three-year OS was 95% vs 88%, respectively (p < 0.001)."
    limitation: "Retrospective database analysis; no molecular data available; data on perineural invasion were insufficient for analysis; the IASLC grading system for nonmucinous adenocarcinoma could not be applied because data collection closed in 2019."
  - source: "IASLC Staging Project, Travis et al. (2024); multivariable analyses"
    finding: "STAS remained an independent prognostic factor after adjusting for age, sex, histologic type, histologic grade, LVI, tumor size, VPI, and extent of resection. In adenocarcinoma, STAS was independent of age, sex, grade, and LVI (RFS: HR 1.49, p = 0.031; OS: HR 1.49, p = 0.032). In other NSCLC, only STAS—not LVI or VPI—was an independent predictor of poor prognosis (RFS: HR 1.91, p = 0.015; OS: HR 2.12, p = 0.017)."
    limitation: "The frequency of STAS varied widely by region (North America 46.2%, Europe 29.4%, rest of world 14.2%, Asia 7%), likely reflecting underreporting rather than true incidence differences, which may have attenuated the observed effect sizes."
  - source: "IASLC Staging Project, Travis et al. (2024); subgroup analyses by extent of resection"
    finding: "STAS was an independent predictor in both lobectomy (RFS: HR 1.43, p = 0.011; OS: HR 1.59, p = 0.011) and sublobar resection (RFS: HR 2.22, p = 0.001; OS: HR 2.43, p = 0.009) cohorts. VPI was an independent predictor or effect modifier in only 2 of 10 RFS subgroup analyses and was not associated with OS in any subgroup after adjustment."
    limitation: "The study was not designed to address implications of STAS for determining optimal surgical management; the relative importance of STAS, LVI, and VPI in determining intrathoracic versus extrathoracic recurrence requires further study."

recommendations:
  - "STAS should be added as a new histologic T descriptor in the Ninth Edition of the TNM Classification of Lung Cancer, alongside the currently recognized VPI, LVI, and perineural invasion (Pn)."
  - "STAS should not be incorporated into measurement of tumor size, because measuring the maximum distance of furthest STAS cells is challenging due to frequent collapse of peritumoral alveolar parenchyma, and STAS cannot be appreciated by CT imaging."
  - "STAS should be recorded as present, absent, or unknown, following the format of UICC and AJCC recommendations for other histologic descriptors, without grading of extent."
  - "STAS should be documented systematically in pathology reports of resected lung cancers and become a standard element in lung cancer synoptic reports worldwide, as already recommended by the College of American Pathologists and the International Collaboration on Cancer Reporting."
  - "STAS should not be used as a clinical descriptor because it is a microscopic finding that cannot be identified on CT imaging; small biopsy or cytology specimens should not be obtained to identify STAS."

open_questions:
  - "Whether STAS could affect stage groupings similar to VPI, and whether it should be incorporated into proposals for stage groupings in future editions of the TNM classification."
  - "Whether the identification of STAS might influence the extent of surgery (lobectomy vs sublobar resection), as this study was not designed to address surgical management implications."
  - "How STAS might be incorporated into the R classification for residual tumor."
  - "The relative importance of STAS versus VPI and LVI in determining intrathoracic versus extrathoracic recurrence, as suggested by TRACERx data showing STAS is associated with increased risk only of intrathoracic recurrence."
  - "Whether the prognostic significance of STAS extends to neuroendocrine lung tumors, including typical carcinoid, atypical carcinoid, large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma, and small cell carcinoma, which are the subject of a separate IASLC subcommittee analysis."

perspective: >-
  The IASLC ninth edition database provides the largest cohort to date—4061
  patients with pathologic stage I R0 NSCLC from around the world—evaluating
  STAS in the context of routine clinical practice. The consistent finding that
  STAS is an independent prognostic factor across all analyses, regardless of
  histologic type or extent of resection, strongly supports its inclusion as a
  histologic descriptor. Notably, STAS and LVI appear to be stronger predictors
  of outcome than VPI in these analyses, which is logical because VPI can only
  be found in tumors in contact with the visceral pleura, whereas STAS can be
  observed in tumors deep within the alveolar parenchyma. The observation that
  STAS was found less often in central T2 tumors is also logical, as tumors
  partially or completely surrounded by a bronchus have less interface with
  adjacent alveolar parenchyma. While the data are not yet sufficient to
  include STAS in stage grouping, adding it as a histologic descriptor will
  elevate its recognition worldwide, enabling collection of carefully annotated
  data for the Tenth Edition TNM classification to better understand the
  relative impact of STAS, LVI, and VPI on lung cancer staging.

bottom_line: >-
  In 4061 stage I R0 NSCLC cases, STAS independently predicted worse
  recurrence-free and overall survival across all histologic types and
  resection extents, supporting its addition as a histologic T descriptor.
